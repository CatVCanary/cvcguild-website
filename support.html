The case for a place at the table

A little game can change someone's whole day.

Imagine a family encountering the Royal Game of Ur for the first time. A visitor makes a move; a volunteer explains why a four-thousand-year-old board still fascinates us. Suddenly an unfamiliar culture has become a conversation rather than a date in a textbook.

That encounter has no extra charge at our tables. We ask visitors to bring curiosity, not supplies or specialized knowledge. We explain what the evidence supports, where a historical game's rules have been reconstructed, and why human beings have kept gathering around games across continents and centuries.

We also create the practical welcome that makes an encounter possible: places to sit, room for a quiet conversation, and hospitality for the volunteers, vendors, and performers who keep a living-history event functioning. We believe cultural education should be something people can actually participate in.

Where your support goes

Help us care for the things that make teaching possible.

01 · A place to participate

Comfortable, accessible seating

Our present pavilion has room for around 22; we aim to expand toward 55 seats, including six accessible places. Donations help with dependable chairs, shade, cushioning, and the storage and transport those improvements require.

02 · History in hand

Durable teaching games

Historical boards are handled, moved and played, not displayed behind glass. Gifts help us build and repair reproduction sets, replace lost pieces, protect fragile items, and prepare clear materials that distinguish historical evidence from modern rules.

03 · A classroom that travels

The pavilion, trailers and weather protection

Our traveling classroom needs canvas care, repairs, safe storage, protective covers, transport, and regular maintenance. These practical costs are essential to keeping public lessons available season after season.

04 · A durable future

A pavilion and teaching cart that last

We are raising support for more resilient pavilion infrastructure and a compact mobile teaching cart. They would expand where and how we can teach while reducing the burden of setting up the full encampment. These are goals, not funded or purchased projects.

We have no paid staff. Directors and volunteers are not paid wages or speaker fees, and Guild members do not pay dues. Contributions support the actual costs of running and maintaining the Foundation's work, including necessary administration and operations—not personal compensation.

Beyond a monetary gift

Your resources, experience or connections may be exactly what we need.

Good woodwork, safe tools, canvas and sewing expertise, weather-resistant supplies, accessibility experience, and historical research support can solve problems money alone does not. Please ask before delivering materials: our space and transport are limited, and we only accept items we can actually use.

We are also seeking the donated temporary use of roughly one genuinely unused, flood-safe acre for two enclosed trailers and low-impact restoration work. We are not looking for rent or land that would displace tenants, farms, or someone's livelihood. Any agreement would require suitable permissions and insurance before occupying the land.

Giving with confidence

Questions worth asking before you give.

Who receives my donation?

The intended recipient is CAT V CANARY FOUNDATION, a Washington 501(c)(3) nonprofit, EIN 33-4166947. The Venmo route is associated with @CvCfoundation; always confirm the recipient inside Venmo before completing a transfer.

Is my donation tax-deductible?

Eligibility depends on applicable tax law and your circumstances. Keep your payment records and contact us if you need a written acknowledgment. For gifts of $250 or more, federal substantiation rules generally require a contemporaneous written acknowledgment from the organization.

Does the Foundation pay its leaders?

No. We are entirely volunteer-run and do not pay salaries or speaking fees to our officers, directors, or volunteers. Organizational costs such as food for volunteers, repairs, equipment, transport and administration still need funding.

Will my employer match a Venmo donation?

Not necessarily. Employers set their own eligible payment routes and matching rules. Search the Foundation in your workplace giving platform or ask your benefits team before assuming a direct Venmo payment qualifies.

Can I sponsor a particular improvement?

We welcome conversations about seating, games, a durable pavilion, the mobile teaching cart, and other practical needs. Contact us first so we can confirm current costs, intended use, and any conditions before you commit.
