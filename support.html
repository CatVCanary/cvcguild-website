A gift with a purpose

Keep history at the table

Every visitor who sits down at our game house benefits from an extraordinary amount of quiet preparation: volunteers who teach, boards and pieces that can withstand hundreds of hands, canvas that must be safely dried and repaired, chairs and tables that travel, trailers that protect the collection, and meals that sustain the people doing the work.

Cat v. Canary Foundation is a 501(c)(3) educational nonprofit with no paid staff. Financial gifts support an accessible, free-to-participate public teaching program—not private salaries. Event gate admission, if charged, is separate and controlled by the organizer.

More places at the table

Repair and expand seating, including accessible positions. The Guild aspires to grow its capacity from about 22 seats toward 55 with six accessible places.

Teaching sets that endure

Make and replace durable boards, engraved game pieces, storage cases, instructional materials, and historical-game reproductions.

Weatherproof and ready to travel

Care for canvas pavilions, wooden structures, trailers, protective storage, tools, and the repairs that keep the classroom safe and usable.

Other forms of generosity

Money is not the only useful gift

A donated tool, a useful piece of furniture, experienced sewing or canvas repair, safe storage materials, skilled woodwork, a contact at a historical institution, or help finding temporary space can remove a genuine obstacle. Please discuss material gifts with us before arranging delivery; not every item can be safely transported or stored.

We are also seeking donated temporary use of approximately one genuinely unused, flood-safe acre for our two enclosed equipment trailers and low-impact restoration work. We do not want to displace tenants, productive agriculture, or another source of landowner income. Read the land-host request.

Build something that can outlast us

A lasting home for a traveling collection

The longer aim is a permanent base where the historical game collection can be preserved, restored, organized, and eventually passed to future stewards of the Foundation. We are exploring donor support, land gifts, and responsible partnerships; no property acquisition or conservation partnership should be assumed complete.

A pavilion is a visible thing, but its greater value is the recurring invitation it makes possible. Your support creates more opportunities for people who might never visit a conventional museum to feel part of history.
