The Cat, the Canary, and the argument that never ended

This page is playful fiction. The characters’ escapades are not presented as historical fact; the Foundation’s public teaching is real.

The Cat insisted the final move was obvious. The Canary insisted the rules were obvious, which was not remotely the same thing. Between them lay a game board older than either of their family trees and a disagreement now entering its third century, by their accounting.

A traveler appeared at the pavilion entrance. The Cat offered a chair. The Canary offered a lecture. From behind a stack of cushions, Bunnie offered a far more practical thing: a board already set for a new game.

By the second turn the traveler had learned what both rivals had forgotten. Games are not monuments to one person’s genius; they belong to whoever gathers to play them. The Canary announced this had been his point all along. The Cat requested written evidence.

They are still debating the matter. The table is still open.

The reality behind the story

A fictional company for a very real purpose

The Cat, Canary, and Bunnie give the traveling house its sense of theatrical mischief. Our volunteers bring the real scholarship, practical kindness, and care for historical material. The imagined household is an invitation into a genuine cross-cultural teaching collection, not a claim that all the games share one place or era of origin.
