More Than a Game House

Comfort, access, food, hydration, and help for our working community are not extras. They are part of the mission.

Our second classroom

Hospitality is how inclusion becomes real

A history lesson is only accessible if people can comfortably participate in it. Our Guild’s game house is furnished as a place to settle in, not merely a display to walk past. A table gives strangers something to do together; shade and seating make it possible to stay, concentrate, and belong.

We cannot remove every barrier in a temporary faire environment, and capacity varies by site. We do make thoughtful, practical choices—sharing information, listening to access needs, and building a culture in which asking for a chair, a quieter moment, or help is ordinary.

A place to sit

We work toward comfortable, varied seating and more accessible places at the table. Our longer-term seating goal is about 55 people, including six accessible positions; these are goals, not guaranteed current capacity.

Food, water, and shade

During faire, Guild volunteers receive meals, hydration, and access to camp facilities. When site rules and resources permit, we extend practical hospitality to other badged participants.

A little help for a working vendor

A solo vendor may need ten minutes to eat or step away. When feasible, a volunteer can welcome customers and maintain a friendly presence—not sell merchandise or answer specialized product questions.

The people behind the booths

A faire is a community at work

Artists, entertainers, vendors, and volunteers often spend long hours helping visitors have an extraordinary day. The smallest basic needs can be hardest to meet when someone is responsible for a booth or performance on their own. An accessible drink, clean water, soap, a shaded chair, or a brief respite can change that day.

We see this service as part of the educational mission rather than a distraction from it. Communities have always relied on mutual care. We can teach that principle through the way our own game house operates.

Participation without a price of admission to the Guild

Make participation possible, not just permitted

Our volunteers do not pay dues, and the Foundation lends costumes to reduce the cost of participating in living history. Approved expenses are organizational responsibilities rather than personal obligations. We are an all-volunteer team, and the camp is for adults age 21 and over. We welcome interest in future youth programming, but no unsupervised youth camp participation is offered.

Our cultural outlook is deliberately open: woman-led, LGBTQ+ inclusive, disability-conscious, and friendly to people who learn best through structured interaction. Visitors remain subject to the host event’s policies and physical conditions.
