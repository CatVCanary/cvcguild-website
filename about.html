The Foundation

Making history accessible through play, participation, and community

Cat v. Canary Foundation is a small, entirely volunteer-run 501(c)(3) educational nonprofit based in Lynnwood, Washington. Its primary public program, the Cat v. Canary Guild, is an immersive traveling encampment presented in an imagined 1570 setting. Visitors encounter a welcoming household of itinerant game enthusiasts whose collection spans centuries and continents.

We do not imply that every game was played in 1570. The encampment is our theatrical setting; each game has its own history, cultural origin, and interpretation. In this way the historical setting helps visitors approach real evidence without confusing performance with scholarship.

Research and honest interpretation

We examine historical games and their context, recreate playable boards and pieces, and distinguish original evidence from reasonable modern reconstructions.

Accessible participation

No special costume, admission fee to Guild activities, game expertise, or prior invitation is needed to join a public lesson. Event organizers may charge their own gate admission.

Community through practical care

We pair learning with shade, seating, water, food, costume lending, and support for people who make public history possible.

Why games matter

Small objects. Vast human stories.

A board game is an unusually intimate kind of historical source. It asks how people made decisions, considered luck, argued over rules, practiced strategy, entertained children, and met others on common ground. Boards and pieces connect the worlds of archaeology, mathematics, craftsmanship, commerce, and social history.

Our approach is participatory. Rather than asking visitors to memorize names and dates, we let them encounter evidence through curiosity. A person can learn about an ancient culture through a move they make themselves—and may remember that encounter long after the event ends.

Who belongs

The table makes room for more than one kind of learner

The Guild is woman-led, explicitly LGBTQ+ inclusive, welcoming of newcomers, and mindful that crowded festivals can be overwhelming. A game offers predictable turns and a common subject to discuss; for quiet or introverted visitors, that structure can make interaction easier than a typical open-ended social gathering.

We offer volunteer costume lending and do not charge volunteer dues or expect members to fund organizational supplies themselves. Approved organizational expenses are reimbursed, so income need not determine who can contribute. Our regular camp is a 21-and-over environment; a separate supervised youth-interest pathway is a developing aspiration, not an open enrollment program.

How we operate

A volunteer-led organization with practical responsibilities

The Foundation has no paid staff. Directors, officers, and volunteers receive no salaries or personal performance fees for their service. Running a traveling game house still costs money: large weatherproof canvas, accessible furnishings, storage, vehicles, repairs, educational supplies, and volunteer meals require ongoing care.

Our aim is to preserve the games, the teaching practices, and the inclusive culture of the Guild beyond any one director or season. The more secure the infrastructure becomes, the easier it is for future volunteers to continue that work.
