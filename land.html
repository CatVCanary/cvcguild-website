A Little Land, a Big Purpose

We seek donated temporary use of genuinely unused land so the traveling classroom can be cared for between events.

A little space, a lasting impact

Looking for roughly one usable acre

We seek a no-cost, written temporary land-use arrangement for approximately two to six years in or around the Monroe–Snohomish–Sultan area of Washington. Our preferred site is genuinely unused, reasonably level, flood-safe, accessible by our two enclosed trailers, and appropriate under local regulations for quiet nonprofit storage and maintenance.

We are not seeking rental listings or asking anyone to give up productive farm ground, displace a tenant, or forgo rental or business income. An existing, surplus clearing or otherwise unused area may be ideal if access, environmental constraints, and legal use all check out.

What would be there

Two enclosed cargo trailers (approximately 7×14 and 7×16 feet), equipment storage, occasional canvas inspection and drying, wooden furniture maintenance, and small volunteer work sessions.

What would not be there

No one residing on the land, no public festival, no permanent buildings, and no assumed permission to clear habitat or build infrastructure.

What must be settled first

A written permission arrangement, site rules, necessary land-use approvals, and appropriate insurance before the Guild moves in or begins any activities.

What an owner could offer

A useful corner rather than a grand estate

An approximately one-acre usable area, legal trailer entrance and turnaround, dry and relatively level ground, and year-round access sufficient for seasonal preparations. We prefer existing suitable ground rather than new clearing; good drainage and freedom from active flood, wetland, or channel-migration hazards are important. River access would be a welcome bonus, never a requirement.

Our work involves keeping historical canvas clean and dry, maintaining wooden frames and game sets without chemical treatments, and preparing equipment for occasional event transport. Volunteers would keep the agreed space tidy and remove stored equipment at the end of the arrangement under written terms. Actual permitted uses must be checked for the specific parcel.

Why an introduction matters

A donated temporary base protects the teaching program

A traveling pavilion is large, and improper storage can damage historic-style canvas, wooden structures, and game collections that are costly for a small nonprofit to replace. A stable, legally suitable home base would help us spend more time teaching and less time coping with avoidable deterioration.

Our long-term hope is a permanent educational home. Temporary donated use is one achievable step, not a claim that property, funding, or a partnership has already been secured. Nor do we promise that merely allowing the use of land is automatically a tax-deductible gift; owners should seek appropriate professional guidance.
