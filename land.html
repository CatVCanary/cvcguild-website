Ever wanted a little Renaissance game house in your back pasture?

Not a public faire—with crowds or traffic—but the quiet, behind-the-scenes home of the artists and historians who make one possible. A corner of an old farm, a wooded clearing, or unused acreage could make an extraordinary difference.

We're looking for roughly one acre of usable, flood-safe ground for 2–6 years, ideally donated or available at very low cost.

Every traveling game house needs somewhere to rest its wagons.

Cat v. Canary Guild is the public teaching program of Cat v. Canary Foundation, a volunteer-run 501(c)(3) nonprofit. At Renaissance faires, we invite visitors to sit and learn ancient and historical board games. Behind that welcome are two trailers of equipment, large canvas pavilions, wooden furniture, teaching sets, and the work of keeping everything safe and ready for the next season.

We need a quiet, practical place to maintain and preserve that collection while we pursue a permanent home. We're particularly interested in suitable land around Monroe, Snohomish, Pilchuck, Sultan, and nearby areas.

What we'd use the land for

  • Temporary setup of large canvas pavilions for cleaning, drying, inspection, and preservation.
  • Chemical-free care and refurbishment of wooden frames, furnishings, and historical game equipment.
  • Parking and accessing our two enclosed equipment trailers (approximately 7×14 and 7×16 feet).
  • Occasional small member work sessions and equipment preparation—not public events.
  • Lawful year-round access for the trailers and periodic maintenance.

What a landowner can expect

  • No residence, permanent buildings, or permanent changes to the land as part of this temporary request.
  • Very little routine traffic, with more activity during June preparation, July move-out, and August return and cleanup.
  • Respect for privacy, agreed site rules, and conservation-minded care.
  • Removal of our belongings and temporary structures when the arrangement ends, with the agreed area returned to its original condition.
  • Written permission and suitable insurance arranged before use begins.
Location and safety matter. We need reasonably level, flood-safe upland with safe legal trailer access. Nearby safe, lawful river access would be welcome for occasional recreation and artistic inspiration, but the river need not be on the property.
Insurance and permissions come first. We will work with a potential host on written terms, site rules, and appropriate coverage, including any agreed additional-insured requirements. No land use begins until permissions and coverage are settled.

Why temporary land matters—and the longer view

Land acquisition can take years. Our Guild has an approximately $20,000 annual operating budget, not a ready-made land purchase fund. A donated or low-cost temporary host arrangement would give us breathing room to preserve equipment and continue free teaching while longer-term options develop.

We are also exploring a permanent home, potentially through donated land, donor-supported acquisition, or conservation-minded partnerships. Any possible conservation or tribal partnership remains exploratory—not an established agreement. Suitable unrestricted upland for Guild operations would need to be compatible with lawful conservation of surrounding habitat.

A land gift, affordable lease, introduction to an owner, or a serious conversation about stewardship could help. We are not asking a property owner to host a public festival.
