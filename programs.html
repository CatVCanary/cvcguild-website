Historical games, taught by playing

At the Cat v. Canary Guild, visitors don't need to know the rules before sitting down. Our volunteer guides introduce each game, explain its cultural and historical context, and help newcomers begin playing.

Games from around the world

Our teaching collection includes Senet from ancient Egypt, the Royal Game of Ur from Mesopotamia, Roman Duodecim Scripta, Chinese Xiangqi, the Maya-associated Puluc, Hnefatafl and Brandubh, Nine Men's Morris, Alquerque, and Liar's Dice.

For games whose original rules are not fully preserved, we explain the difference between historical evidence and modern reconstruction. We encourage curiosity rather than pretending every question has a certain answer.

Living-history demonstrations

We use handmade and reproduced game boards, pieces, and a furnished traveling pavilion to help visitors imagine how games brought people together. Lessons are interactive, informal, and free to participants.

Community and accessibility

The pavilion offers a place to sit, cool down, and connect. We welcome visitors who need a quieter moment during a busy faire, as well as performers and vendors taking a break.

Where to find us

Our public appearances are announced on the Guild's event section. Event admission and schedules are managed by the organizers of each faire; participation in Guild demonstrations is free.
